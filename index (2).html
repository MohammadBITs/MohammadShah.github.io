Cryogenic propellant storage for an Earth–Mars transit

Independent research, technical report dated March 23, 2026

Problem
Liquid hydrogen warms and boils off during a long trip. Over a 240-day transit, heat leaking into the tank costs thousands of kilograms of propellant.
What I did
Built a Python model of a 2 m spherical tank with a time-varying solar environment, then swept insulation layers and cryocooler power to find the lowest total system mass.
Result
The best design was 11 insulation layers plus 110 W of active cooling. Boil-off fell from 5,241 kg to 583 kg (about 89%), for a total system mass near 350 kg. Assuming a constant 300 K outer temperature underestimated passive boil-off by about a factor of two.

Python, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, SolidWorks, Fusion 360. Code and report on GitHub
